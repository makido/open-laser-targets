USTERTAG PRACTICE REPRODUCTIONS - 17 NOVEMBER 2026

Two target families, each at 7.5 m practice distance, with a two-page PDF
(target then scoring companion), vector SVG pair and OLTS 0.5 definition.
The combined PDF contains only the two target pages. These reproductions were approved for the public library on 17 November 2026.

P25 AUSZAHLUNGSSTICH / UST25B

The user measured the outer printed scoring square at 248 x 248 mm, excluding
the paper margin. At 7.5 / 25 scale, that square prints at 74.4 x 74.4 mm.
The square is centred over the existing PP25 precision-target artwork. Its
500 mm outer circle prints at 150 mm diameter; the 248 mm square obscures
the centre. All exposed circular rings are artwork only and score zero.

Scores: 3 inside the outer square, 4 inside the black field, 5 inside the coat
of arms. Everything outside the 248 mm square is zero. Higher nested scores
win, and a boundary counts inside its zone under OLTS point-score rules.

The photo was rectified using the four printed border corners. The black
field is traced as approximately 157.65 x 158.50 mm; the white/red shield is
approximately 69.5 mm wide and 79.8 mm high. These internal measurements are
photo-derived, not independent ruler measurements or official dimensions.
The shield's lower curves are cubic fits to the visible contour. Maximum
nearest-curve deviation from the selected photo samples is
0.535 mm at original size (0.161 mm printed).
That is a fitting residual, not a claim about total physical accuracy:
perspective, paper curvature, annotation and ruler uncertainty remain.
Shot holes, patches and handwritten dimension arrows are not reproduced.
The red coat-of-arms stripes follow the photograph; lettering is newly typeset.

P50 GRUPPENWETTKAMPF / UST50B

The user measured the white/red nine-zone rectangle at 148 x 198 mm.
The official full image depicts 5..10, and the organiser's text also lists
only 0 and 5..10. The user clarified that 1..4 continue outside the shown area,
then explicitly requested constant-width nested-rectangle extrapolation.
This practice honours that instruction without treating the repair-centre edge
as the scoring limit. The discrepancy with the website is not resolved.

Visible 10..5 proportions suggest width increments of half the nine-zone
width: 74 mm. The nine-zone height is the 198 mm measured anchor. The visible
9..5 heights increase by roughly three quarters of that height: 148.5 mm.
The ten-zone height is inferred as 60% of the nine height: 118.8 mm.
These proportions are inferred from the small official raster image and
checked visually against the repair-centre photo; only the 148 x 198 mm
nine rectangle is directly measured. Zones 1..4 continue the inferred width
AND height increments. The user confirmed that the black background extends
through zone 1, with white boundary lines and white labels in the outer zones.

LAYOUT AND ENCODING

Both use a 210 x 279.4 mm shared frame centred on A4 or US Letter at 100%.
The delivered PDFs are A4; centred actual-size Letter rendering was checked.
Each page includes horizontal and vertical 50 mm calibration segments.
Three 24 mm ArUco markers retain 4 mm white surrounds. Each QR area is 50 mm
including the quiet zone. No scoring region is clipped or hidden by symbols.
The P50 centre is shifted to x=98 mm to leave its QR outside the outer zone.
The P25 uses the full-page marker arrangement to clear its backing disc. The scoring companions contain coloured
regions and boundaries; normal target lettering and coat-of-arms colour are
omitted from the scoring regions themselves.

Distinct practice IDs avoid reusing existing library identities:
UST25B: QR version 13, 292 URL bytes, markers [29, 24, 12].
UST50B: QR version 16, 427 URL bytes, markers [45, 41, 15].

Format/layout checks, all zone-colour comparisons, outside-zero tests, unique
identities, QR decoding and marker detection passed. All four final A4 pages
and four centred Letter renditions were checked. Physical printing and app
recognition have not been tested. Geometry provenance remains distinct from
format conformance. A future geometry change needs a fresh target identity.
